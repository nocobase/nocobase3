Add `@nocobase/lifecycle`, record lifecycles for applications: a business record keeps its state in one of its own fields, every change is a transition declared in source, and the effects a transition owes run after it commits

`defineLifecycle()` declares states, transitions with `from`, `to`, `guard`, `validate`, `accept`, `route` and `set`, `onEnter` effects, and triggers that fire a transition on records idle in a state for longer than a parameter allows; it rejects an unknown state, a multi-target transition without a route, or a trigger waiting where its transition cannot start when the module loads. `defineEffect()` declares an effect with retries, a stable idempotency key per run, and `onSuccess`/`onFailure` transitions, fired with the effect's result or its error as input. `LifecycleRuntime.fire()` checks the state, writes the record, a transition log entry and the effect runs it owes in one transaction, guarded by a conditional update on the state and on a version every transition increments, so concurrent transitions of one record, self-transitions included, cannot both commit, and dispatches the effects after commit; an attempt `recover()` took back cannot record a result, an effect's outcome and the transition it continues with commit together, and a run interrupted on every attempt becomes `dead` rather than running forever; `runTriggers()` finds idle records by a query rather than a timer per record, and `recover()` hands over queued runs and takes back attempts whose process stopped. A guard refuses with a message or `{ code, message }`, which `available()`, `can()` and the refusal from `fire()` report as blockers, and `validate` may return a list of field problems; `addGuard()` lets other code veto transitions without touching the definition; and `create()` starts a record in an initial state with a log entry and the state's `onEnter` effects, so a record's history starts at its creation. `onTransition` writes related rows inside a transition's transaction, and `on()` tells listeners about `completed`, `entered` and `announce` events after commit. States and transitions carry titles, `final` and `meta`, `from` takes `'*'` or `{ except }`, the definition refuses unreachable states and states with no way out that are not final, and `toMermaid()` draws a description as a state diagram. Effects take a backoff factor, a cap, `shouldRetry` and `timeoutMs`; `fire()` takes a `requestId` that makes a repeated request a replay; and `listEffectRuns()`, `retryRun()`, `cancelRun()` and `prune()` let an operator see, rerun, stop and clear effect runs. An attempt whose outcome could not be recorded goes back in the queue after its backoff rather than staying claimed, `reclaim()` hands over again, without waiting for a restart, attempts whose lease expired and queued runs whose dispatch was lost, a retry counts its attempts on from the ones before so a late attempt cannot pass for a new one, and a trigger pages past the records its guard refuses so they cannot starve the ones behind them. `@nocobase/lifecycle/jobs` provides `createLifecycleJobs()`, the dispatcher that runs effects as `@nocobase/jobs` jobs, waits out backoffs, and sweeps triggers and expired attempts on a schedule. `@nocobase/lifecycle/hono` provides `createLifecycleRoutes()`, the standard routes to view a record, fire its transitions and operate its effect runs, and `@nocobase/lifecycle/react` provides `createLifecycleHook()`, configured once with a plugin's transport and routes so a page calls a single hook, built on `useLifecycle()` over any transport with `request()`, such as an application's API client. `createRepositoryLifecycleStore()` persists through `@nocobase/db` Repositories, and hands registered service factories the transaction's connection so a guard can read other collections inside the transition — on SQLite a read elsewhere would wait for the only connection, and `@nocobase/lifecycle/testing` runs a lifecycle on a memory store with a fake clock, in-process effects and simulated failures, so waits, retries and continuations are unit tests.

Add `@nocobase/app-plugin-lifecycle-example`, which runs two lifecycles on the application's database and jobs service behind pages shaped like the products they model: a help desk where customers file tickets, agents take them from a queue and reply, a ticket left waiting closes itself and a customer can reopen it, with the conversation read from the transition log and each email's delivery shown under its reply; and expense reports with itemized lines, routed by amount to automatic approval or to the manager and finance, which approvers approve, send back or reject with a reason, an idle manager is passed over for theirs, and payment runs as an effect that marks the report paid with its reference. One person can act as every role, and each record shows its transitions and effect runs. The examples template registers the plugin's server and client.

Add `@nocobase/app-plugin-office-flows-example`, two office processes built from six lifecycles: a data usage request whose form asks follow-up questions by answer, passes three managers and reaches an acceptance that cannot return it once an extraction task exists nor finish while one is open, with extraction tasks created for each period and moved off weekends and the 2026 public holidays; and an incoming document approved by the office and distributed to departments, whose clerks countersign and may hand it to execution teams, executors or assisting departments, with each person reminded once per document and level and every distribution traced on the document. The examples template registers the plugin's server and client.

Let a second layer of state run while a record waits in a state, such as the tasks of an approval stage or a conversation with an assistant. `runtime.transaction(work)` runs one transaction across every registered lifecycle: `tx.fire()`, `tx.create()` and `tx.read()` check what `fire()` and `create()` check, `tx.handle` writes rows of the caller's own, and `tx.afterCommit()` schedules a best-effort callback; events, effects and callbacks follow the commit and none of them happen on a rollback, and a failure after a transition wrote anything rolls the whole transaction back even if the caller catches it. `onEnterState` and `onLeaveState` declare hooks that run in the transaction of every transition entering or leaving a state, `runtime.create()` included, a self-transition running both; they and `onTransition` receive the transaction as `tx`. A state definition may carry its own `onEnterState` and `onLeaveState`, which run before the lifecycle's for that state, so a module can provide a whole state for a business to list. A transition declared `manual: false` is fired only by server code: the standard routes refuse it with `NOT_MANUAL`, `available()` leaves it out, `can()` refuses it with a `manual` blocker, `announce` skips it and `toMermaid()` marks it. The memory store now takes back only what a failed transaction wrote, rather than restoring a snapshot that also undid an effect run claimed outside it, joins a transaction opened inside one, and lists a collection's rows with `records()`.

Add two second layers written by hand to the example plugin, each keeping its rounds in rows of its own while a record waits in one state: a visa whose applicant and officer exchange material in rounds, and an order whose assistant asks the planner to choose before it re-plans. Neither moves the record until it concludes, and a late answer for a stay that has already ended is refused.

Add `@nocobase/app-plugin-approval`, approvals on record lifecycles. The business record stays a plain lifecycle that waits in one state while an approval decides: `defineApproval()` turns stages, who decides each and how their answers add up into a lifecycle of its own, so that an approval run is a record whose states are the stages, and `approval.state()` provides the waiting state, which starts a run on entering and cancels it on leaving early. The run ends by firing one of the exit transitions the business declares, in the transaction of the answer that ended it. Changes a stage proposes to the content are kept on the run and written to the record only when the run ends approved or returned, through `approval.settle`. `ApprovalService` answers, claims, releases, hands over, adds signers, consults, asks for material, returns, revises, reassigns and lists tasks, each in one transaction, and stage policies decide a stage by everyone, anyone, the first, a threshold, a pool, a sequence or line by line. The plugin's migration creates `approvalRuns`, `approvalTasks` and `approvalEvents`, which the layer reads and writes through the lifecycle's Repository store transaction.

Add `@nocobase/app-plugin-approval-example`, 28 approval scenarios on `@nocobase/app-plugin-approval` running on the application's database: leave with rule versions, collaboration by first answer, any approval, countersignature and committee vote, purchases by amount and in parallel across departments, contracts with returns, revisions, hand-overs, added signers, consultations and procurement pools, itemized expense claims, payments executed in installments, budget grants, supplier onboarding with a registry check and deposit, employee onboarding sub-processes, product launches, notices that take effect once confirmed, and orders that wait for their payment provider. An approval center under **Approvals** presents them as business applications with a to-do center across them, routes previewed before a request is sent, the actions each person may take, approval flows and request history; an approval lab adds samples, the clock, the administrator's control of the organization and of simulated provider outages, and each record's transitions and effect runs. The examples template registers the approval plugin and the example.
