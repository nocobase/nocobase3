Add `@nocobase/lifecycle`, record lifecycles for applications: a business record keeps its state in one of its own fields, every change is a transition declared in source, and the effects a transition owes run after it commits

`defineLifecycle()` declares states, transitions with `from`, `to`, `guard`, `validate`, `accept`, `route` and `set`, `onEnter` effects, and triggers that fire a transition on records idle in a state for longer than a parameter allows; it rejects an unknown state, a multi-target transition without a route, or a trigger waiting where its transition cannot start when the module loads. `defineEffect()` declares an effect with retries, a stable idempotency key per run, and `onSuccess`/`onFailure` transitions, fired with the effect's result or its error as input; an effect that throws an `EffectFailure` fails at once, unless it asks to be retried, and hands its `code` and `details` to `onFailure`, so the next transition branches on a code rather than on a message. `LifecycleRuntime.fire()` checks the state, writes the record, a transition log entry and the effect runs it owes in one transaction, guarded by a conditional update on the state and on a version every transition increments, so concurrent transitions of one record, self-transitions included, cannot both commit, and dispatches the effects after commit; an attempt `recover()` took back cannot record a result, an effect's outcome and the transition it continues with commit together, and a run interrupted on every attempt becomes `dead` rather than running forever; `runTriggers()` finds idle records by a query rather than a timer per record, and `recover()` hands over queued runs and takes back attempts whose process stopped. A guard refuses with a message or `{ code, message, kind }`, which `available()`, `can()` and the refusal from `fire()` report as blockers, each a `permission` refusal unless the guard marks it a `precondition` that nobody may get past until the record changes; `validate` may return a list of field problems, checked before the guards in `fire()` and in a `can()` given input, so a guard there reads only input that passed, while `available()`, `view()` and a `can()` without input ask the guards with `{}`, which a guard must answer as well; `can()` takes the input a button would send and answers its problems or blockers without throwing, for a transition whose answer depends on its input; `addGuard()` lets other code veto transitions without touching the definition; and `create()` starts a record in an initial state with a log entry and the state's `onEnter` effects, so a record's history starts at its creation, after checking the definition's `create`: `validate` over the values, then `guard` over the values, the state and the actor, refusing as a transition does, so who may create what is one rule for every route, import and script. `fire()` and `create()` take a `transaction` to join — the handle an `onTransition` receives, or the `@nocobase/db` connection of the caller's own transaction — nested in it as a savepoint, so a parent creates its children and a child moves its parent on in one commit, a refusal undoes only the nested call, and effects and listeners wait for the outermost commit and are dropped on rollback. `onTransition` writes related rows inside a transition's transaction, and `on()` tells listeners about `completed`, `entered` and `announce` events after commit. States and transitions carry titles, `final` and `meta`, `from` takes `'*'` or `{ except }`, the definition refuses unreachable states and states with no way out that are not final, and `toMermaid()` draws a description as a state diagram. Effects take a backoff factor, a cap, `shouldRetry` and `timeoutMs`; `fire()` takes a `requestId` that makes a repeated request a replay, refuses one already used for another transition on the record, or by another actor, with `REQUEST_REUSED` rather than replaying it, and refuses an empty one, or one starting with `$`, which the library keeps for its own log entries, with `INVALID_REQUEST_ID`; and `listEffectRuns()`, `retryRun()`, `cancelRun()` and `prune()` let an operator see, rerun, stop and clear effect runs; a continuation is logged under a request id naming its run and outcome, so a run continues its record at most once per outcome, and `retryRun()` refuses with `RUN_SETTLED` a run whose `onFailure` already moved the record on, or waits to, unless given `force`, the only settled runs it refuses, so a caller checks the record before retrying a dead or cancelled one. A continuation is nested in the transaction that records its effect's outcome, so a refusal, its own or one raised by a lifecycle call in its `onTransition`, rolls back the whole continuation and leaves the outcome recorded; a refusal saying its own record moved on leaves nothing to follow, while any other refusal, such as a parent a lifecycle call in its `onTransition` could not move yet, an old definition in a rolling deploy or a bug in `set`, keeps the continuation on the run as `continuation`, which `reclaim()` tries again once due, backing off from a minute to an hour and taking at most a configurable batch per sweep, `continueRun()` tries at once, `listEffectRuns({ continuationPending: true })` lists and `prune()` keeps, and neither runs the effect again, which only a `CONFLICT` or a failure that is not a refusal does; its listeners and effects come before those of what its `onTransition` started; and an `EffectFailure` whose `details` are not JSON is recorded as an ordinary failure without them. An attempt whose outcome could not be recorded goes back in the queue after its backoff rather than staying claimed, `reclaim()` hands over again, without waiting for a restart, attempts whose lease expired and queued runs whose dispatch was lost, a retry counts its attempts on from the ones before so a late attempt cannot pass for a new one, and a trigger pages past the records its guard refuses so they cannot starve the ones behind them, passing over its own refusals quietly and logging one a lifecycle call inside the transition raised. `@nocobase/lifecycle/jobs` provides `createLifecycleJobs()`, the dispatcher that runs effects as `@nocobase/jobs` jobs, waits out backoffs, and sweeps triggers and expired attempts on a schedule. The library ships no HTTP routes, which belong to the plugin under its namespace and declared for the application's API document: `lifecycleErrorFields()` turns a refusal into the fields of the standard error body for an `ApiError`, with the blockers and problems in `metadata`, answering a guard refusal whose blockers are all preconditions with `FAILED_PRECONDITION` and any other with `PERMISSION_DENIED`, and `lifecycleDescriptionView()` builds what a page draws a lifecycle from. `@nocobase/lifecycle/react` provides `createLifecycleHook()`, configured once with a plugin's transport and base path so a page calls a single hook, built on `useLifecycle()` over any transport with `request()`, such as an application's API client; it calls the record routes the guide lists, reads `{ data }`, and rejects a refusal with the standard body's `reason`, blockers and problems. `createRepositoryLifecycleStore()` persists through `@nocobase/db` Repositories, and hands registered service factories the transaction's connection so a guard can read other collections inside the transition, since on SQLite a read elsewhere would wait for the only connection. The plugin that owns the lifecycles declares the log tables in its own migration: the transition log keeps the caller's nullable `requestId` and a not-null `requestKey`, the `requestId` or `$v:<version>` without one, under a unique index on `(lifecycle, recordId, requestKey)`, a plain index that behaves the same on every dialect, Oracle, Dameng and MSSQL included, and the effect runs keep a nullable `continuation` and a nullable `continuationDueAt` the sweep finds the due ones by. A plugin declares `@nocobase/lifecycle` as a peer and the application provides it, because plugins share one runtime and recognise its errors by identity. `@nocobase/lifecycle/testing` runs a lifecycle on a memory store with a fake clock, in-process effects and simulated failures, so waits, retries and continuations are unit tests: its `InProcessDispatcher` holds a retry until the fake clock passes its backoff, and `runDue()` runs it, unless `retries: 'immediate'` asks for every retry at once, and the memory store's rollback undoes only the failed transaction's own writes, so a run a worker claimed meanwhile stays claimed.

Add `@nocobase/app-plugin-lifecycle-example`, which runs two lifecycles on the application's database and jobs service behind pages shaped like the products they model: a help desk where customers file tickets, agents take them from a queue and reply, a ticket left waiting closes itself and a customer can reopen it, with the conversation read from the transition log and each email's delivery shown under its reply; and expense reports with itemized lines, routed by amount to automatic approval or to the manager and finance, which approvers approve, send back or reject with a reason, an idle manager is passed over for theirs, and payment runs as an effect that marks the report paid with its reference. One person can act as every role, and each record shows its transitions and effect runs, with buttons to retry, cancel or continue a run. Its routes live under `/api/lifecycleExample`, page their lists, change only the fields a `PATCH` sends and move the report's version on with every edit, so a submit decided on the report before an edit landed is refused with `409` rather than routed on the old amount, answer `{ data }`, refuse in the standard error body, a refusal the record's state causes with `400 FAILED_PRECONDITION` and one the person causes with `403`, and are declared in the API document. The examples template registers the plugin's server and client.

Add `@nocobase/app-plugin-office-flows-example`, two office processes built from six lifecycles: a data usage request whose form asks follow-up questions by answer, passes three managers and reaches an acceptance that cannot return it once an extraction task exists nor finish while one is open, with extraction tasks created for each period and moved off weekends and the 2026 public holidays; and an incoming document approved by the office and distributed to departments, whose clerks countersign and may hand it to execution teams, executors or assisting departments, with each person reminded once per document and level and every distribution traced on the document. Its routes live under `/api/officeFlowsExample`, page their lists, take only the fields a state lets the persona edit and change only those a `PATCH` sends, answer `{ data }`, refuse in the standard error body, a refusal the record's state causes with `400 FAILED_PRECONDITION`, one the person causes with `403`, and a missing or unknown field the request names with a field violation, and are declared in the API document. The examples template registers the plugin's server and client.
